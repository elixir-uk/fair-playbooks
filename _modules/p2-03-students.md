> **v1 – drafted from Contentathon #3 (1 July 2026).** Grounded in real objections from students, PhDs and the RDM professionals who support them. To be tested and refined at the October rollouts.

**Who you're advocating to.** Someone who does the hands-on work and generates the data. They were never formally taught FAIR, and it isn't graded. What they are probably thinking: "What? Another thing I need to do? My supervisor knows nothing about this, so where do I start?"

## What stops them

The barriers they voted most important:

| Their words | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| "I've no time alongside my thesis." | Real or perceived, it's the top worry. |
| "There are no instructions – and is this even my responsibility as a PhD?" | No lab support, and sometimes opposition from the supervisor. |
| "It's dry admin, not 'real' research." | FAIR competes with the science they came to do. |
| "My supervisor doesn't do it – why would I?" | Going against the current is hard. |
| "My data's public – what if there's something wrong?" | Fear of exposure plays as big a part as apathy. |

## What works (the hooks that answer them)

The framings the room judged strongest:

- **Save your future self.** Organised, catalogued data makes writing the thesis far easier, and the data is easier to explain to a supervisor. Sell it on the time it saves.
- **Piggyback on what they already do.** Most students already back up their work religiously. Frame FAIR as "the backup you already do, plus a little cataloguing and metadata." They don't need a new habit, only a better version of one they have.
- **"FAIR enough for your thesis."** Push back on the idea that "proper FAIR" is scary and complicated. Give them a short checklist of what their uni or community needs, and stop there.
- **Make it about them.** "Helps future reusers" doesn't motivate a student. "Helps you, now" does.
- The reframes that opened up the discussion: "How might we make a time-poor student see FAIR as saving them stress?" · "Give them something practical without having to understand all of FAIR" · "Make FAIR a normal part of research, not a bolt-on."

## How to deliver it

**The organise-vs-chaos race.** One group keeps its data organised as it goes and the other doesn't. Then both groups have to "write it up" and share it. Which team is faster and less stressed? The activity is hands-on, and it shows the "saves you time" pitch without you having to argue it. Whatever game you use, keep it simple and present FAIR as something that reduces stress rather than another demand.

## What they say

> "There's too much information on FAIR so I have no idea where to get started."
> "We'll worry about FAIR at the end – first, let's play with our data."
> "It's not exciting – it'll never be exciting."

Use these quotes word for word. They show that your pitch should lead with ease and relevance. Leave the principles out.
