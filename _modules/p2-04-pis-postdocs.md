> **v1 – drafted from Contentathon #3 (1 July 2026).** Grounded in real objections from PIs, senior postdocs and the RDM professionals who support them. To be tested and refined at the October rollouts.

**Who you're advocating to.** People who drive the science, are always short of time, and think about FAIR mainly when a funder or journal makes them. What they are probably thinking: "Everyone around me seems to be winging it."

## What stops them

The barriers they voted most important:

- "I only engage when a funder or journal forces me." (voted the top barrier by the room)
- "I / my group don't have time."
- "I need dedicated staff with niche expertise – and not on fixed-term contracts, so there's legacy."
- "Who will pay for the extra time?"
- "I'll lose control of my data, or get scooped."
- "I'm not sure where to start – so many resources and opinions."

The themes underneath are time, funds, and expert hands-on support with continuity. The room also accepted that some people won't share. Don't spend your energy trying to convert them. Support the people who are willing.

## What works (the hooks that answer them)

The room voted recognition the biggest lever, followed by resource and expertise, then better processes.

- **Recognition.** Credit FAIR work the way we credit papers, through CV metrics, badges, promotion criteria and awards ("champion your champions"). "Metadata is a love note to the future."
- **Time saved for them.** Ask: "How might we make a time-poor PI see FAIR as saving them time?" One answer is data-management software that links the plan to the data throughout the grant, so there is no panic at the end.
- **Expert support.** Take the work off them. Cost "FAIR gurus" and shared research facilities into the grant at the writing stage. Researchers can't do everything, and an expert will do it better and faster.
- **A bigger frame.** The room loved a Galaxy talk that described FAIR as "an organisational transformation – a way of thinking and working, like the scientific method."

## How to deliver it

Recognition-led framings work best as one strong slide plus a story, such as a data-reuse success from your own institute. The "no time" angle pairs well with a quick demo: "here's a stack of lab books, find one specific result". The demo is described in the [decision-makers module](../p2-05-decision-makers/) and works for PIs too.

## What they say

> "Now funders are asking me to fill in a box I can fill with AI, then go back to doing 'real' research."
> "FAIR only comes onto my radar at publication."
> "I need dedicated staff with the niche expertise – not fixed-term."
