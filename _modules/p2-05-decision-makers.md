> **v1 – drafted from Contentathon #3 (1 July 2026).** Grounded in real objections from RDM leads, librarians and research-office decision-makers. To be tested and refined at the October rollouts.

**Who you're advocating to.** Someone who holds budget, policy or staff time. They are under pressure from funders but wary of who will pay for the upkeep. They see themselves as "the bridge that frees up researchers' time" and don't want to be the enforcer.

## What stops them

The barriers they voted most important:

- "How do you assess a dataset's value, to prioritise where to invest limited resources?" (clearly the room's top barrier)
- "Fragmentation of support across library / data steward / local teams – whose budget?"
- "Researchers say they've no time – do I push or not?"
- "We'd need 100% FAIR compliance", when FAIR is better understood as a journey.
- "What are the hidden costs – supporting secondary users, lost commercial opportunity?"
- "No consequence to non-compliance." · "I need evidence / ROI before committing staff or money."

## What works (the hooks that answer them)

**On prioritisation (how do we value data?):**

- "FAIR data raises others' confidence in the research → more citation and reach."
- "Metadata is a love note to the future." · "Making data FAIR is like giving archaeologists a map – spend less time digging, more time discovering."
- Turn the barrier into the session. Run an activity that defines all the ways a research object can have value.

**On "researchers have no time":**

- "There's no future value in data that isn't understandable – even to the team who made it." Present FAIR as credibility insurance in the age of AI slop.
- Bring in expert support early, at the grant-writing stage. Train PIs on data-management software that links the plan to the data across the whole grant.

**For the one exec slide:** the cost of not making data FAIR · FOMO ("competitor institute X is already doing A, B, C") · funder and compliance risk · responsible-AI readiness · data as an exploitable asset · getting "data risk" onto the institutional risk register so that someone senior is responsible for it.

## How to deliver it

The room rated these two demos highest:

- **The lab-books demo.** Hand people a stack of lab books and ask them to find one specific result. Then ask them to estimate how much research funding (£) those notebooks represent. People feel the problem straight away.
- **Lego Metadata for Reproducibility.** One team builds with instructions and metadata, the other without. See who finishes. ([doi.org/10.36399/gla.pubs.196477](https://doi.org/10.36399/gla.pubs.196477))

## What they say

> "Perfect is the enemy of good – we don't want 100% FAIRness straight away."
> "Data re-use isn't considered novel research by funders, so why put effort in?"
> "UK-wide consistent approaches – I'm looking at you, BioFAIR."
